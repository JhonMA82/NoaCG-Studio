## Explicit disagreements - the table vs designs the owner passed

Stated per the plan's own clause (explain before altering the standard, both directions). The
table still binds for the R3 round; these rows are what the owner re-ratifies with open eyes.

1. **The primary size floor (4.6%, ~50px) indicts most of the catalog and most of what the
   owner aired.** 312 of 489 shipped designs carry a primary line under 50px, and 14 of the 17
   airable sweep cells - including all four outright AIRs (X-02, X-34, X-43, X-49) - would
   have been STOPPED by it. The classifier calls the LARGEST informational line primary, so a
   corner scoreboard's 32px score or a ticker's 30px crawl is held to the headline floor. The
   R3 round runs this floor as ratified; if the read shows the models converging on
   50px+ primaries that the owner then airs, the floor is doing its job - if delivered-clean
   collapses or the owner calls the type oversized, this row is the number to revisit.
2. **The 5% safe-area inset stops two aired corner scoreboards** (X-32, X-34): a top-corner
   scoreboard sits closer than 96px to the edge. The catalog's own 119px convention passes;
   the question for the owner is whether a compact corner graphic is exempt or the inset has a
   per-type exception.
3. **The collision rule fires on 21 shipped designs** (credits rolls and tickers dominate:
   cr01-cr13, tk02, ig34): a settled frame of a rolling/crawling design can legitimately show
   text crossing a rule mid-travel, which the instrument cannot tell from a static collision.
   On the seven sweep types it caught 7 owner-failed cells with ZERO false positives, so it
   blocks in the round as ruled; the credits/ticker catalog hits are R4 product-validator
   calibration work, not round blockers.
4. **The secondary floor at 1.85% (20px) is the only floor the catalog already meets** (17 of
   443, all corner bugs at their own authored 16px - the type-floor gate's known exception).
   The 2.2% warning band covers 392 of 443, i.e. nearly every shipped supporting line sits in
   the "prefer bigger" band; the band is advisory by design.

## Reading the table

- A BLOCKING rule with false positives above zero would have stopped a graphic the owner
  aired: each is named so the owner can re-ratify or adjust the number with open eyes.
- True positives count cells the machine DELIVERED clean and the owner rejected - the
  §22.1 escape classes, now measured.
- Warning rules never stop a delivery; their counts show what the advisory channel says.
