# Creative Mode - the three owner rulings, 2026-07-31

The two smoke rounds (`SMOKE-2026-07-31.md`, `SMOKE-2026-07-31-vs-lt.md`) ended on three
questions only the owner could answer. All three were ruled on 2026-07-31; this file records
the ruling, what it changed in the code, and - for the first one - what measuring it revealed
that the ruling itself could not have anticipated.

## 1. Opaque full-frame backdrops - **spec-driven: forbidden on overlays, measured on boards**

An overlay covering the canvas is never what was asked for, so it is a deterministic refusal.
A full-frame board covering the canvas is the job, so it is measured instead.

- **The gate** (`src/ai/creative/style.ts` `stripFrameFlood`, applied when the spec says
  `fullFrame: false`): a rule that BOTH fills the frame and paints it opaque loses its fill and
  keeps its paint. Clamp, not reject - the `stripHidingDeclarations` precedent. Which half
  survives is the whole ruling: dropping the paint leaves an invisible full-frame box with the
  content sprayed across the canvas (a broken graphic), while dropping the fill leaves the panel
  painted at content size in its zone (the design the model meant, minus the flood). Only the
  combination is touched - a full-bleed element that paints nothing and an opaque panel at
  content size are ordinary design and are left alone. Pinned in `e2e/creative-pilot.spec.ts`
  against the REAL flooding CSS from the archived bracket round, with the full-frame mutation
  twin.
- **The measurement** (`scripts/creative-plate-visibility.mjs`, FREE): the rig already
  composites every hold frame over one known plate, so the plate is a measuring instrument -
  the fraction of pixels still matching it is the fraction of the picture the graphic did not
  take. The floor is CALIBRATED against the catalog's own designs, like criterion 6's copy
  line, never a number anyone invented. `scripts/creative-plate.mjs` holds the plate definition
  the rig and the measurement share, so the reference and the capture cannot drift.

### What running the measurement on the archived rounds showed (all free)

1. **The floor is per CATEGORY or it is nothing.** Over the lower-third and versus references
   together the catalog minimum is 0.0% - vs02 is a full-frame match-up that legitimately
   covers every pixel - and a floor of zero excuses every flood there is. Grouped: lower third
   **91.2%** (89 designs), versus **0.0%** (2 designs, both total covers).
2. **The instrument reads true.** Ordinary straps land at 95-99% plate visible; the reference
   plate matches the captured plate with no systematic offset.
3. **It reads the OTHER end too, exactly.** 10 of 32 lower-third frames are pixel-identical to
   the bare plate: those graphics painted *nothing*. That is the empty-capture failure round 1
   noticed by eye, now an exact measurement rather than an impression of a dark PNG.
4. **The versus "flood" is not a coverage defect at all.** Both catalog versus designs cover
   100% of the frame, so a covering versus card is normal broadcast practice, and the
   measurement correctly declines to flag it. What made those frames bad was round 2's other
   words - "near-illegible small text" - which is CONTRAST, a dimension the runtime bench
   already owns. The calibration is what stopped a plausible-sounding threshold from being
   invented here.
5. **The flag the ruling keys on is unreliable, and that is the finding that matters most.**
   14 of 16 lower-third specs came back `fullFrame: true` - for graphics whose own family word
   was "strap" and whose zone was "bottom-left". Read as "does this graphic own the whole
   frame", the flag sounds like permission to use the canvas and the answer is always yes. The
   consequence is bigger than the gate: `scaffold.ts` anchors a zone graphic and CENTRES a
   full-frame one, so those straps were never anchored where their own spec said. The stage-5
   schema now asks which of two concrete broadcast objects is being made ("if a director would
   call for this over a live camera, it is FALSE") instead of asking for permission.
   **The next round measures whether the reframe worked: the gate's fire-rate and the
   `fullFrame` rate on lower thirds are both readings, not assumptions.**

## 2. Critique acceptance - **accept when no worse than the base**

`styleRepairFromCritique` required the repaired template to pass FULL validation, so on an
already-invalid base it structurally could not land: 1 repair landed out of 20 across both
rounds, and §11 criterion 8 could not be measured at all. The rule is now `noWorseThan`
(`src/ai/creative/pipeline.ts`, exported and pinned): a repair lands if it is clean, or if it
introduces no NEW error rule and no more errors than the base it started from. The property
that mattered - *a critique may improve a result, never break one* - is kept; the property that
was accidental - *only a perfect result counts* - is gone.
