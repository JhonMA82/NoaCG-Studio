### Reading these three numbers correctly

**1. Engineering validity is roughly flat, not transformed.** Against the SAME candidate's
chunks in the last pass (qwen3-30b; the gemma chunks are not comparable and are excluded):

| arm C validity | last pass | this round |
|---|---|---|
| lower third | 62% | 50% |
| versus | 73% | 69% |

A 12-point drop on 8 results is one result, and versus is flat. **Do not read either as a
finding.** The plausible mechanism - text now renders at real broadcast sizes, so
`bench-overflow` and `bench-stress` can fire on layouts that previously had nothing in the box
to overflow - is consistent with the direction but is not evidenced by numbers this small.
It is stated here as a hypothesis for the next round to test, not as a result.

Arm A's structural satisfaction on lower thirds also moved, 12% -> 57%, and it is worth saying
plainly that **nothing in the repair explains it**: arm A is the frozen control, the scaffold and
the `fullFrame` derivation are staged-path only, and the one shared change (the field-reachability
gate) can only ADD findings, never remove them. On 7-8 completed results that is 1 -> 4, which is
what run-to-run variance on a stochastic coder looks like. Recorded, not claimed.

**2. Arm A's structural satisfaction on versus is 8%** - one result in twelve carries every part
the brief asked for, against arm C's 85% and D's 100%. This is not new: the control was at 17%
on versus in the last pass, so a full-frame match-up card has always been the shape it satisfies
worst. What is new is the cost gap - the control now costs **ten times** arm C per attempt
($0.0160 vs $0.0016) - and it lost a run to the known `coder-next` malformed-response. On lower
thirds the control is the more reliable arm (100% validity); on versus it is not.

**3. Concept diversity holds** - 92% on versus for both staged arms, top composition family at
46%/25% (the 50% cross-brief tripwire is not tripped). LT diversity on arm C is 50% with `strap`
at 100%, which is the right answer for a category where every brief IS a strap.

## What this round does and does not settle

**Settles:** the three faults the repair targeted no longer appear in the frames where they were
measured. The pipeline now produces graphics that carry their own data at their own type sizes,
which the last pass could not. The one place the bench was genuinely BLIND - structural
satisfaction counting a hidden data holder as the required part present - now has a gate that

these results as invalid before too, under a different signature.

human pairwise review, and a 6-frame agent sample is not it. Nothing here overturns the §11 NO-GO
- it removes the reason the last round could not be reviewed at all.

## Reproduction

node scripts/creative-pilot-bench.mjs --route=openrouter:qwen/qwen3-30b-a3b-instruct-2507 \
  --coder-route=openrouter:qwen/qwen3-coder-next \

Each chunk needs its OWN `--out`: `pilot.json` is written at the END of a run, so a reused
directory loses the previous ledger while keeping its artifacts. The dev-bench server must be
running on this checkout's port and stopped before any e2e run reuses it.
