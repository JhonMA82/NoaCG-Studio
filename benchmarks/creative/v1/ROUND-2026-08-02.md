# Creative Mode - the clamp round, 2026-08-02

Three lower-third rounds testing four platform changes, after the owner reviewed the
2026-08-01 re-run's frames and was not impressed. **$0.278 total.** Versus was deliberately
NOT run: by the third round the evidence was clear enough that another category would have
bought nothing.

| round | changes in it | spend |
|---|---|---|
| `lt` | the 2026-08-01 baseline (repair work only) | $0.0810 |
| `lt2` | + type-ladder floor, safe-area clamp, critique acceptance loosened | $0.0958 |

## What each change did

| | arm C valid | arm C struct | style landed | diversity | arm D valid | D repairs landed |

### The list fix is the one that shows in the frames

The intent stage types ordinary fields `list` constantly, and after the unrenderable-field
repair gave every list field its own row container, a name-and-role strap compiled into a
TWO-ROW TABLE: both values hidden in data holders, drawn as `.creative-cell` at one flat 26px,
the emphasis ladder bypassed entirely. **21 staged runs across three rounds rendered that way
with no region declaring repeating content anywhere.**

That is the honest answer to "why does it look plain", and it was self-inflicted - introduced
by the cause-4 repair while fixing something else. A list is made a list by repeating
STRUCTURE, which both the spec and the intent declare; absent both, the field is now a line.
Recompiling every staged spec from all three rounds: rows-without-repetition **21 -> 0**,
graphics carrying real text spans up to **36 of 57**. In the frames, type went from flat cell
size to broadcast size.

### The type floor works, and is half the job

Region text below the ladder's size for its emphasis is now raised; bigger is left alone.
Sizes are right in the round-3 frames. What it does NOT fix, visible in the same frames:
**hierarchy is flat** (name and role render at the same size, so nothing ranks) and **contrast
is poor** (low-contrast text on the plate). A floor guarantees big enough. It says nothing
about differentiated or legible, and those are the next two things the eye notices.

### The safe-area clamp is unproven

No measured effect either way. It was suspected of causing arm D's `bench-entrance` findings
and CLEARED by measurement: the failing box measures 1900x36 with and without the clamp
applied. Kept, on the grounds that capping to title-safe is correct regardless, but nothing
here is evidence that it helps.

### The critique acceptance change was TRIED AND REVERTED

Dropping the veto on findings whose rule the base did not carry was well-argued - the critique
found something on 88-100% of results and its repair was refused 17 times in 20, and a
composition that moves genuinely does trade findings between rules.

**It bought nothing.** The landing rate stayed at 25% in all three rounds, identical before and
after, so the refusals were never coming from that veto. Meanwhile arm D's validity fell 75% ->
63% -> 13% while arm C improved. One round of 8 is not proof of causation and there was no
measured upside to weigh against it, so the rule is back and carries a note recording the
attempt. **If revisited: find where repairs are ACTUALLY refused first** - the patch gate in
`style.ts` also refuses, and is the more likely source.

## Where the staged path stands

Arm C is meaningfully better than three rounds ago - validity 50 -> 63%, concept diversity
50 -> 88%, style landing 63 -> 75%, at $0.0011 per attempt against the control's $0.0075. The
frames are correctly sized, correctly placed, and carry their own data.

They are still PLAIN: flat hierarchy, weak contrast, no plate, no accent. Clamps fixed
everything a clamp can fix. Hierarchy, contrast, plate and accent are not out-of-range values
to clamp - they are composition, and the model has no vocabulary to compose from. It writes
every pixel of CSS against a text description of a scaffold.

**The catalog already owns that vocabulary** (`templates/pack4/skin.ts`: `panelCss`,
`accentCss`, `labelCss`, `dividerCss`, `measureCss`), and the creative path cannot reach it.
Whether it should is the §3.3 question and an owner ruling, because §4's anti-anchoring rule
says catalog knowledge reaches CREATE "only as machine-readable constraints" - a parameterized
panel emitter is arguably exactly that, but it is not this report's call to make.
