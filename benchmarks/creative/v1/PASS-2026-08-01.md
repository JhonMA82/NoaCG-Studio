## The human read (2026-08-01, Mirko, 8 frames) - this is the result that counts

The scorecard above is gates. This is criterion 4, and it points the other way.

Eight frames were put in front of the owner: matched arm pairs plus the extremes the plate
measurement had already flagged. The verdict, per frame:

| frame | arm | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| q30 lt-three-line (the best staged result: critique repair landed, valid) | D | not professional |
| q30 lt-explicit-adapt | C | "just a grey square - not a graphic" |
| q30 lt-long-name-portrait | D | unidentifiable |
| gm lt-original-auto | C | a black screen |
| **q30 lt-long-name-portrait** | **A** | **good lower third** |
| q30 vs-editorial-create | D | a white page |
| **q30 vs-sports-classic** | **A** | **ok - but the same as the template we already ship** |
| q30 vs-sports-classic | C | not any good |

**0 of 6 staged frames usable; 2 of 2 frozen-control frames usable.** On §11's own rule that
is the NO-GO condition - "a CREATE that loses the pairwise review has no production story
regardless of the rest." It does not need the full 155-frame blind review to be actionable:
the review exists to resolve close calls, and this was not close.

**Criterion 2 passing is not the good news it reads as.** Structural satisfaction measures
that the required parts are PRESENT in the DOM. A frame the owner called "just a grey square"
and a white page with one candidate's name in default-link blue both scored structurally
complete. Presence is not a graphic. This is the F5 inversion the two-scorecard rule exists
for, and it caught this report's own headline: the pass was first summarized as "the staged
arms carry the structure", which is true and misleading in the same breath.

**The more useful half of the verdict is about arm A.** The one good versus card "looks
exactly the same as we have in the templates". The frozen control is usable because it
reproduces the catalog - which is the anchoring problem (F3, the §4 anti-anchoring rule) that
Creative Mode was written to solve, now OBSERVED rather than inferred, and consistent with
criterion 6's spot check (arm A results sit 0.08-0.10 from their nearest catalog design
against a copy line of 0.06). So the honest state is: **the control is usable but is not
creating; the staged path is creating but is not usable.** Neither currently delivers what
this feature is for.

**Sampling honesty:** 8 of 155 frames, and they were not drawn neutrally - two were the worst
floods by measurement, chosen to be informative rather than representative. A fuller look
could soften the 6-0 ratio. It is unlikely to reverse the direction, and no measurement in
this report contradicts it.

**What this changes:**
- Do NOT build the pairwise gallery yet. Blind-ranking 155 frames would spend reviewer time
  re-deriving a result that was not close.
- Do NOT pay for another round yet. The staged failures are not taste, they are breakage - a
  style patch landing empty or not at all, straps compiling as centred full-frame slabs, and
  results with no visible text scoring structurally complete. All three are diagnosable for
  FREE from the generated HTML/CSS already in the archive.

## The diagnosis (2026-08-01, free - read off the archived output, no tokens spent)

Following the human read, the staged failures were traced in the generated HTML/CSS. Over the
**84 staged (C/D) attempts, 75 completed**, they fall into three platform-side classes. None of
them is taste.

| class | count | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **lifecycle** (`bench-entrance` + `bench-replay`) | **30 / 75** | 86% of ALL staged invalidity (30 of 35 invalid runs). The third cause of that signature - style patches re-implementing the lifecycle as classes nothing toggles - identified during this session and deliberately left unfixed to keep the matrix comparable. It is by far the biggest single lever |
| **gate refused the patch** | 11 / 75 | The style patch trips a wall, so the bare scaffold renders. That is the frame the owner rejected as "Manchester United and the AI icon" - placeholder text and an empty logo slot. **"The scaffold is a safe floor" is true for validity and false for airworthiness** |
| **`fullFrame` wrong** | 22 / 28 lower thirds | Disables ruling 1's backdrop clamp. CONFIRMED mechanically on lt-explicit-adapt: `.creative-box` fills the frame transparently and then a REGION - `.creative-r-background`, `position:absolute`, 100%x100%, `linear-gradient(--panel-bg -> --accent)`, `z-index:1` - floods it. That rule matches `stripFrameFlood` exactly on both halves (frame-filling geometry + opaque paint); it was skipped only because the spec claimed full frame |

### The result that matters more than any of them

**39 of the 75 completed staged runs were valid AND gap-free** - and of the six staged frames
the owner actually judged, **four were in that clean set, and all four were rejected**
(lt-three-line D "not professional", lt-long-name-portrait D "I don't know what that is",
vs-editorial-create D "a white page", vs-sports-classic C "not any good").

That was first read as "repair is necessary but not sufficient - nothing here shows the staged
path can design well even when nothing is broken." **The second diagnosis pass below withdraws
that reading.** Two further causes were found, each larger than any of the three above, and
neither is visible to any gate - so "clean" never meant "the pipeline worked". All four of the
clean-set rejects carry at least one of them. What stands is the weaker, still-binding claim:
a re-run must be judged on frames first and gates second.

## The second diagnosis pass (2026-08-01, free) - two causes larger than the first three

Both are staged-path-only, deterministic, and invisible to the validator, the runtime bench and
the structural check. Together they mean **no judged frame in this pass came from a run that
rendered its own data at its own type sizes**, so the pass carries no evidence about whether
scaffold+style can design - only that it has not yet been given the chance.

### Cause 4 - fields that cannot render (48 of 69 staged runs, 88 fields)

`scaffold.ts` binds fields to regions through `spec.regions[].fieldKeys`. Stage 5 frequently
returns `fieldKeys: []` on *every* region (vs-sports-classic C: all four regions empty). The
unclaimed-field fallback (`scaffold.ts:366`) exists for exactly this, but it **excludes roles
`list` and `hidden`** - so any field the intent stage typed that way becomes a hidden
`.noacg-data-source` holder with nothing that draws it. Two more edges compound it:

- the list runtime reads **only the first** list field (`listFieldId`), so a second `list` field
  can never appear;
- its host element `#creative-rows` is emitted only when a region declares `repeating: true`,
  while the runtime is emitted whenever any field has `role: 'list'` - **26 of 55** runs shipped
  the runtime with no host, so `rebuildRows()` returns on its first guard.

vs-sports-classic C is the whole class in one file: four fields, all four hidden holders, three
regions empty comments, one rows host fed by f0 alone. The frame is "Manchester United | ⭐"
centred on an empty plate. Its record: `ok=true`, zero error rules, zero warnings, zero
structural gaps, `styleApplied=true`. vs-editorial-create D is the same defect and is the "white
page" - the visible `5` is the one field that got a slot.

**Why every gate passed it:** structural satisfaction asks whether the required part is present
in the DOM, and a hidden holder *is* present. This is criterion 2's blind spot stated exactly.
The repo already owns the technique that catches it - `scripts/field-coverage.mjs` drives every
field to a sentinel and re-reads the screen - and it is not wired to the creative bench.

Where staged text did appear, it is often the style stage re-adding the field as a visible span
around the scaffold's hidden holder (lt-three-line D). That produces **duplicate `id="fN"`** in
one document, which the applyPolish-class gate accepted while its stated job is preserving field
ids. The model was patching around the platform, and the gate did not notice either half.

### Cause 5 - unitless `font-size: calc()` deletes the typography (59 of 76 staged CSS files)

The scaffold writes `font-size: calc(26px * var(--scale) * var(--type-scale))`. The style stage
imitates the pattern and **drops the unit**: `calc(48 * var(--scale) * var(--type-scale))`. That
is not a valid `<length>`, so the browser discards the declaration and the text falls back to
the default ~16px in a 1920x1080 frame. 218 such declarations across 59 of 76 staged stylesheets.

The arm split is total and identifies the cause as the scaffold's own pattern rather than the
models:

| arm | CSS files with a unitless `font-size: calc()` |
|---|---|
| A (frozen control) | 0 / 38 |
| B (neutral-skeleton coder) | 0 / 41 |
| C (staged) | 29 / 40 |
| D (staged + critique) | 30 / 36 |

The coder arms write plain `font-size: 48px` and are unaffected. This is the mechanical
explanation for "not professional" and "unidentifiable" on frames that were otherwise gate-clean:
the design's entire type hierarchy silently never applied. A declaration-validity check in the
patch gate catches the whole class - the round-h clamp precedent, one level lower.

### What these two change

- The "repair is not sufficient" conclusion is **withdrawn**. It rested on four judged frames
  that are all in cause 4, cause 5, or both. There is no clean-pipeline sample to judge yet.
- The repair order changes: causes 4 and 5 come **before** the lifecycle class. They are cheaper,
  fully deterministic, and they are what stands between the pass and an interpretable frame.
- The §3.3 scaffold-vs-layout-IR question cannot be settled on this pass either way. Note though
  that empty `fieldKeys` is precisely the "model fills a structural binding badly" failure a
  bounded deterministic composer removes by construction - so cause 4 is evidence *for* moving
  binding out of the model, and the cheap version of that move is the fallback fix, not a new IR.

## Findings

### 1. Ruling 1's gate is correct and is being bypassed 80% of the time

`spec.layout.fullFrame` decides whether the overlay backdrop clamp applies. **24 of 30
lower-third specs (80%) claimed full frame** - for graphics the same spec calls a "strap"
anchored "bottom-left". The stage-5 schema was reworded before this pass to ask which of two
concrete broadcast objects is being made; the rate did not move (it was 14/16 = 88% before).
So the clamp almost never fires on the category it was written for, and the measurement shows
the consequence directly: **8 of 60 lower-third frames sit below the catalog floor of 91.2%
plate visible** - floods that ruling 1 would have refused had the flag been honest.

This is now the top blocker for the staged arms' visual quality, and it is a platform problem
rather than a prompt problem: two rewordings have failed to move it. The flag is also
load-bearing for placement (`scaffold.ts` centres a full-frame graphic instead of anchoring
it), so an unreliable flag mis-places straps even when no flood follows.

### 2. Plate visibility does not discriminate on versus, and that is the honest answer

Both catalog versus designs cover 100% of the frame, so the calibrated versus floor is
0.000% and coverage says nothing about a versus card's quality. What made round 2's versus
frames bad was legibility over a full-frame wash - contrast, which the runtime bench already
owns. The per-category calibration is what stopped a plausible-sounding coverage threshold
from being invented here.

### 3. 31 of 155 frames painted nothing at all

15 of 60 lower-third and 16 of 95 versus frames are pixel-identical to the bare plate. Against
a deterministic plate this is exact, not an impression: those graphics rendered nothing, so
they measure the entrance rather than the design and must be excluded from the pairwise review
rather than ranked as bad designs.

### 4. One signature, now three causes

The `bench-entrance` + `bench-replay` pair recurred a third way. Round 2 fixed hidden
structure; this session fixed CSS `transition` on scaffold elements (found on the first paid
brief, fixed and pinned before the matrix ran). It then recurred again as state classes
(`.visible` / `.fade-out` / `.playing`) that nothing toggles, plus `z-index: -1` on text -
the same "model re-implements the lifecycle" family round 2 named. **The pipeline was frozen
at that point** and the remaining 20 briefs ran unchanged: fixing per signature mid-matrix
would have made the chunks incomparable, which is the whole point of a candidate pass.

### 5. Arms A and B are near-replicates across candidates

With split routes both candidates' A/B arms are served by the same `coder-next`, differing
only through the shared intent stage. They are worth their cost as a measurement of
coder-next's stochastic failure rate (6 of 84 coder-arm runs failed to complete), but the real
candidate comparison in this pass is C-vs-C and D-vs-D.

## What this pass says about the candidates

**qwen3-30b is the better candidate on every staged-arm measure** - validity, concept
diversity, and the only one whose critique repairs land at all. gemma-3-12b holds structural
satisfaction (the scaffold does that work regardless of model) but returns one design idea
three times and never produces a landable repair.

Neither is promoted, and the candidate question turns out to be the wrong one to be asking
yet: the human read found the staged path unusable on BOTH candidates. Choosing between two
models is a question worth paying for only once the pipeline they drive produces graphics.

## Next

Diagnosis is DONE (both passes above, no tokens spent). The remaining order, cheapest and most
load-bearing first:

1. **Cause 4 - make every declared field renderable.** Extend the unclaimed-field fallback to
   `list`/`hidden` roles, emit the rows host whenever the list runtime is emitted, and give a
   second list field somewhere to go. Deterministic; no prompt change.
2. **Cause 5 - reject invalid declarations in the patch gate.** A unitless `font-size: calc()`
   is dropped by the browser, so the gate must clamp or repair it. Same pass: refuse a patch
   that duplicates a field id.
3. **Wire the field-coverage technique into the creative bench** (`scripts/field-coverage.mjs`
   drives every field to a sentinel and re-reads the screen). Without it, cause 4 recurs
   silently and criterion 2 keeps certifying hidden holders as satisfied parts.
4. **The lifecycle cause** (30/75) - state classes nothing toggles.
5. **Make `fullFrame` honest** - deterministically, not by a third rewording (two have failed).
6. **Re-run versus + lower third only after 1-5**, and look at the frames before reading any
   gate.
7. The bounded layout IR, then bracket (ruling 3).
8. The pairwise gallery LAST - it resolves close calls, and there is no close call to resolve
   until the staged path produces something worth ranking.

## Reproduction

```
node scripts/creative-pilot-bench.mjs --route=openrouter:qwen/qwen3-30b-a3b-instruct-2507 \
  --coder-route=openrouter:qwen/qwen3-coder-next \
  --critique-route=openrouter:google/gemini-2.5-flash \
  --category=lower-third --only=<1-4 brief ids> --out=<own dir per chunk> --max-cost=0.28
node scripts/creative-catalog-refs.mjs --categories=lower-third,versus --out=<out>/catalog-refs
node scripts/creative-plate-visibility.mjs <frames> --house=<out>/catalog-refs --group=lower-third
npm run bench:sameness -- <out>/catalog-refs
```

Each chunk needs its OWN `--out`: the rig writes `pilot.json` at the end of a run, so a reused
directory loses the previous chunk's ledger while keeping its artifacts.
